Dear Editors of Optimization Letters,

Please consider the manuscript “Pre-Export Row Scaling for Mixed-Integer Programs: Mechanism Attribution and Residual-Budget Contracts” by Mathias Rodríguez Castro.

The manuscript studies when information supplied by a model generator can affect a row-scaling policy, and what numerical guarantees can be attached to the resulting export. Its first result identifies an information-loss mechanism: geometric-mean local row normalization collapses a subsequent extrema-based block statistic, with a bounded extension for near-identity safeguards. Matched controls explain why a coupling-kernel change can otherwise appear to be a block-information benefit.

The second part characterizes joint admissibility of original-unit residual budgets and coefficient windows, including constrained minimax centering, integer-rounding allowances, and exact binary64 power-of-two exports. These are conditional row guarantees, with explicit abstention when the specifications conflict. The article distinguishes them from solver feasibility and runtime.

The computational evidence includes 1,080 fresh matrix exports, a reconstruction of 240 dispatch runs, and 3,456 planned configurations on exactly enumerable binary instances. The artifact includes the unsuccessful cases, the 288 pre-solve abstentions, and an independent checker. The manuscript does not claim a universal scaling advantage: direct budget scaling outperforms the extra centering in one solver, and exact export alone does not ensure solver acceptance.

This concise mathematical and computational study addresses numerical reliability and the interpretation of preprocessing experiments. The main manuscript is accompanied by a supplement and a source/data archive. The author is the sole author, received no external funding, and declares no competing interests. The manuscript discloses the use of AI assistance in derivations, code, analysis, and writing.
