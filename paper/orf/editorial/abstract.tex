Row scaling preserves a mixed-integer programming model in exact arithmetic but
changes the application-unit meaning of absolute feasibility tolerances. We
present a verifiable model-generation procedure integrating original-unit
residual budgets, admissible row-factor selection or explicit abstention,
integer-rounding allowances, finite-export error reserves and independent
returned-point checks. The contribution connects established scaling and
residual-transfer ideas to reproducible software decisions. Evaluation combines
306 hydrothermal dispatch models, kernel-matched and Ruiz controls, enumerated
stress tests on three solvers, and independent batch scheduling. On 24 prospective
parameter scenarios from six known inputs, the budget-aware Euclidean policy
has paired geometric-mean work and complete-process time ratios of 0.422 and
0.797 against no external scaling; candidate cost deterioration reaches 1.02\%
and one reported bound is independently contradicted. A larger frozen study
comprises 3,840 runs on 160 models from 40 historically generated parents and
two solvers. Against conventional Euclidean scaling, paired ratios of penalized
verified-availability time are 0.995 for Gurobi and 1.000 for CPLEX, with
whole-parent intervals containing one; Gurobi complete-process time increases
by 7.9\%. All policies meet the mapped physical budgets in both prospective
samples. CPLEX acceptance is highly sensitive to tiny written-bound residues.
The results support explicit precision specification, selection and verification,
without establishing general acceleration or additional prospective physical
feasibility recovery.
