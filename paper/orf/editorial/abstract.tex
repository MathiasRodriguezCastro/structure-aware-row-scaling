Row scaling preserves a mixed-integer programming model in exact arithmetic but
changes the application-unit meaning of absolute feasibility tolerances. We
develop a model-generation workflow using original-unit residual budgets. It
selects row factors under coefficient limits, reports incompatible requirements,
accounts for integer rounding, checks binary64 export errors during selection,
and independently checks returned points.
Evaluation combines 306 hydrothermal dispatch models, kernel-matched and Ruiz
controls, enumerated stress tests on three solvers, and independent batch scheduling.
With a frozen 100\,m\textsuperscript{3} policy, prospective validation on 24
perturbed scenarios from six known dispatch inputs and three new seeds gives
paired geometric-mean work and complete-process time ratios of 0.422 and 0.797
against no external scaling. Every policy meets the physical budgets; the
budget-aware policy retains one independently contradicted bound and candidate
cost deterioration reaches 1.02\%. Against conventional Euclidean scaling, the
work ratio is 0.931, with a scenario-cluster interval containing one. Excluding
each known parent preserves lower work against the unscaled control, but can
reverse the comparison with Euclidean scaling. An earlier directed six-model
comparison gives a work ratio of 0.317; preparation overhead slows inexpensive
models. Common verified-candidate targets show no clear time advantage over
conventional Euclidean scaling. Batch scheduling verifies budget delivery
without an additional primal benefit. These results support explicit precision
specification and verification, with configuration-dependent performance, rather
than general acceleration or certified mixed-integer optimality.
