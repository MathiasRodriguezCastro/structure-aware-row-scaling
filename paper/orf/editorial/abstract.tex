Row scaling preserves a mixed-integer programming model in exact arithmetic but
changes the application-unit meaning of absolute feasibility tolerances. We
develop a software workflow using original-unit residual budgets during model
generation. It selects admissible row factors under coefficient limits,
reports incompatible requirements, accounts for integer rounding and binary64
export, and independently checks returned points.
Evaluation combines 306 hydrothermal dispatch models, kernel-matched and Ruiz
controls, enumerated stress tests on three solvers, and independent
batch-scheduling models. In a directed comparison on six known dispatch models
and three seeds, the budget-aware Euclidean policy reduces solver work by
68.3\% relative to no external scaling, measured by a paired geometric mean.
This result uses a 100\,m\textsuperscript{3} volume budget, automatic presolve and
integer feasibility tolerance $10^{-9}$ in Gurobi. In that comparison, primal budgets
are satisfied without detected bound contradictions, with candidate cost deterioration
below 0.1\%; these checks do not establish equally certified optimality gaps.
Preparation overhead makes the three inexpensive models slower.
A prospective check on 24 perturbed scenarios from known parents retains lower
work against unscaled controls without clear advantage over conventional Euclidean
scaling, with one budget-aware bound contradiction.
Batch scheduling verifies budget delivery without an additional primal benefit
over conventional Euclidean scaling. Performance benefits remain conditional on
the instance and numerical configuration.
