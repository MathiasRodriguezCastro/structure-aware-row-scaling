Row scaling preserves a mixed-integer programming model in exact arithmetic but
changes the application-unit meaning of absolute feasibility tolerances. We
develop a model-generation workflow using original-unit residual budgets. It
selects row factors under coefficient limits, reports incompatible requirements,
accounts for integer rounding, checks binary64 export errors during selection,
and independently checks returned points.
Evaluation combines 306 hydrothermal dispatch models, kernel-matched and Ruiz
controls, enumerated stress tests on three solvers, and independent batch scheduling.
In a directed comparison on six known dispatch models and three seeds,
budget-aware Euclidean scaling reduces solver work by 68.3\% relative to no
external scaling, measured by a paired geometric mean. This uses a
100\,m\textsuperscript{3} volume budget, automatic presolve and integer feasibility
tolerance $10^{-9}$ in Gurobi. Primal budgets are satisfied without detected bound
contradictions, with candidate cost deterioration below 0.1\%; these checks do not
establish equally certified optimality gaps. Preparation overhead slows the three
inexpensive models. A prospective check on 24 perturbed scenarios from known
parents retains lower work against unscaled controls, with one budget-aware
bound contradiction. Common verified-candidate targets show no clear time
advantage over conventional Euclidean scaling. Batch scheduling verifies budget
delivery without an additional primal benefit. Performance benefits remain
conditional on the instance and numerical configuration.
