Evaluation must distinguish representation quality from computational benefit.
Changing a kernel together with its metadata can misattribute a gain to block
ownership, while solver-side equilibration can reverse an exported condition-number
ranking. Likewise, lower work among completed runs can coexist with a timeout
that reverses the failure-sensitive comparison. The experiments therefore use
matched kernels and strong numerical controls, retain adverse outcomes, and
measure conditioning, effort, complete-process cost and original-unit accuracy
separately (\cref{subsec:results-bridge,subsec:results-operational}).
