\paragraph{Computational validation.}
The evaluation separates conditioning, solver effort, factor admissibility and
verified accuracy (\cref{sec:experiments,sec:results}). It combines 306 dispatch
models, kernel-matched and Ruiz controls, enumerated stress tests on three
solvers, and batch-scheduling transfer. The initial physical audit leaves factors
unchanged; an activation follow-up changes them and supplies exact feasible
witnesses and strong numerical controls. Directed comparisons then test the
favorable budget settings against the unscaled model and account for complete
preparation cost. These are within-family results, not equally certified MILP
gaps. Exact global LP bounds and integer witnesses provide separate reference
intervals, including a certified dispatch optimum (Online Resource~1, Table~S19).
The directed and prospective accuracy comparisons use demand-inversion-free
inputs. Defective historical scenarios are identified and retained as numerical
MIPs, with limited operational interpretation.

Residual transfer and accuracy-based scaling are established practice
\cite{gurobiExternalScaling,pritchett2017scaling}. The software contribution
connects budget declaration, admissible selection or explicit abstention, checked
export and returned-point verification. Its Python selector and isolated C++
integration give identical factors and exports. The tests retain failures,
contradicted bounds and preparation overhead: checked precision and computational
benefit are distinct outcomes.
