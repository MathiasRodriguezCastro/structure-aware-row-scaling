\paragraph{Computational validation.}
The evaluation separates conditioning, solver effort, factor admissibility and
verified accuracy (\cref{sec:experiments,sec:results}). It combines 306 dispatch
models, kernel-matched and Ruiz controls, enumerated stress tests on three
solvers, and batch-scheduling transfer. The initial physical audit leaves factors
unchanged; an activation follow-up changes them and supplies exact feasible
witnesses and strong controls. Directed comparisons test favorable budget
settings against the unscaled model, accounting for preparation cost.
The prospective studies add 24 perturbed scenarios and a larger frozen
3,840-assignment confirmation from 40 historically generated parents
(\cref{subsec:orf-prospective,subsec:orf-large-confirmation}). Every policy meets
the mapped physical budgets in these samples: no additional physical-feasibility
recovery is demonstrated there. Recovery in adversarial and presolve-off tests
is evidence for those selected configurations, not a general operational benefit.
The larger confirmation establishes no primary time advantage over L2.

Residual transfer and accuracy-based scaling are established practice
\cite{gurobiExternalScaling,pritchett2017scaling}. Our software connects budget
declaration, admissible selection or explicit abstention, checked export and
returned-point verification; its Python selector and isolated C++ integration
produce identical factors and exports. These decisions and independent checks,
rather than the transfer identity alone, are the methodological contribution.
The accuracy comparisons use demand-inversion-free inputs. Defective historical
scenarios remain identified as numerical MIPs with limited operational meaning.
Exact LP bounds and integer witnesses supply separate reference intervals,
including one certified dispatch optimum (Online Resource~1, Table~S19).
