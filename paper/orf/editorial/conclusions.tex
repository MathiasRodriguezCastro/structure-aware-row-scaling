\subsection{Conclusions}
The contribution is a reproducible model-generation procedure connecting
original-unit precision requirements to admissible row factors, checked export
and independently verified returned points. Established transfer relations
become explicit selection or abstention decisions, with allowances for integer
rounding and finite export. A nominal solver tolerance alone does not establish
the exported-residual hypothesis required by the conditional guarantee.

Controlled comparisons distinguish conditioning from solver effort, identify a
kernel-confounded metadata interpretation and expose an inactive coupling stage.
Selected adversarial and presolve-off tests demonstrate primal recovery, while
batch scheduling verifies transfer without an additional benefit over matched L2.
The 24-scenario study improves work and time against Base; the larger
3,840-assignment confirmation establishes no primary time advantage over L2
and retains increased Gurobi process cost. Neither prospective sample adds
physical-budget recoveries. A separate sensitivity explains much of CPLEX's
low acceptance by tiny written-bound residues, without changing the registered
criterion or establishing an incremental contractual benefit.

For implementation, the useful deliverable is a traceable precision decision:
retain original units, select admissible factors or report incompatibility,
audit export and returned points, and assess candidate quality and complete
cost alongside successes and failures. Checked primal accuracy and the absence
of detected contradictions do not certify MILP optimality.
