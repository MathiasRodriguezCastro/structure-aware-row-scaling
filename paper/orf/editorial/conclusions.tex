\subsection{Conclusions}
Accuracy-aware model generation connects row factors to requirements in the
application's original units. Residual-budget contracts provide admissible
intervals, incompatibility checks and constrained selection, with separate
allowances for integer rounding and finite export. The implementation can
select a certified sufficient candidate or report abstention; returned-point
verification remains necessary because nominal solver tolerances do not
establish the actual exported-residual hypothesis.

The controlled comparisons on 306 dispatch models distinguish exported
conditioning, subsequent equilibration and solver effort. Kernel matching
identifies a confounded metadata interpretation, while the final-policy
ablation identifies an inactive coupling stage. These results support implementation
audits rather than using a better condition measure as a runtime prediction.

Computational benefits remain conditional. Small adversarial tests and one
presolve-off dispatch case show primal recovery under selected settings;
batch scheduling verifies budget transfer without an additional benefit over
matched L2. The 24-scenario prospective study retains lower work and process
time against Base, but supplies no clear incremental advantage over L2. The
larger 3,840-assignment confirmation likewise establishes no primary time
advantage over L2; Gurobi's complete-process cost is higher. All policies meet
the mapped physical budgets in these prospective samples. Strict written-bound
residuals limit CPLEX acceptance, and missing scaled bound contrasts limit
what the detector can say about solver bounds. Physical-budget and domain
checks do not certify MILP optimality.

The practical contribution is therefore an explicit, reproducible precision
workflow: retain original units, select admissible factors or report
incompatibility, audit export and returned points, and measure candidate
quality and complete-process cost alongside successful and failed targets.
