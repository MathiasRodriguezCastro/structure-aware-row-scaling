\subsection{Larger fixed-policy confirmation}
\label{subsec:orf-large-confirmation}
\paragraph{Frozen design and independent units}
This study compares four fixed policies on 160 models from 40 historically
generated parents, three dispatch formulations and four horizons (24, 72, 168
and 360 hours). Three new seeds and two solvers give 3,840 assignments,
independently replayed in 480 blocks. The 24 assignments of one independently
infeasible model remain diagnostic; the common eligible mask contains 3,816
assignments. Policies, budgets, shuffled execution order and resources are fixed
before comparison, with caps determined by Base-only calibration.
Each model/seed block runs all eight solver/policy calls on the same node.
The 40 parents, rather than seeds or horizons, are the resampling units;
100,000 whole-parent bootstrap draws retain their nested observations and mask.
The parents still share historical components and documented prior policy
exposure. This is a larger within-family test, not independent operational data.

Acceptance combines physical budgets (0.01 MW and 100\,m\textsuperscript{3}),
integer distance at most $10^{-9}$, exact written bounds after integer rounding,
remaining-row backward error at most $10^{-6}$, and a common cost target within
0.1\% of an exactly verified feasible reference. Reference acquisition is
separate; those references are not assumed optimal. Native stopping gaps are
0.1\%, unlike the earlier 1\% study, so the studies are not pooled.

\paragraph{Physical precision and sensitivity of exact-domain acceptance}
Every eligible final point meets the mapped physical budgets under every
policy. Thus this sample demonstrates no additional recovery of physical
feasibility from contracts. CPLEX's low combined acceptance instead primarily
reflects positive written-bound residues: 379/477 final Budget-L2 points and
378/477 L2 points fail exact bounds. Among their positive residues, the medians
are $3.41\times10^{-13}$ and $4.51\times10^{-13}$, and maxima
$2.46\times10^{-6}$ and $3.55\times10^{-6}$, respectively, in the corresponding
variable units. These magnitudes distinguish small domain deviations from
failures of the declared physical budgets.

A separately identified post hoc sensitivity changes only the written-bound
threshold, reusing all saved cost-eligible candidates and keeping every other
check fixed (Table~\ref{tab:orf-large-sensitivity}). Allowing $10^{-9}$ raises
CPLEX target counts to 395 for Budget-L2 and 405 for L2; allowing $10^{-5}$ gives
430 and 431. The sharp increase confirms the influence of exact-domain
acceptance, without identifying an additional Budget-L2 benefit. These are
diagnostic algebraic thresholds, not physical standards. The registered zero
threshold and all primary time comparisons remain unchanged. Per-candidate
audit timestamps were not saved for alternative thresholds, so no alternative
PAR2 times are inferred. Online Resource~1 gives both solvers' full sensitivity
and residue distributions; Online Resource~2 independently reconstructs the
domain arithmetic from saved vectors and written bounds.

The scaled bound detector has no applicable contrasts because the Base
reference is not certified feasible for the rounded scaled representations.
Zero flags there cannot validate solver bounds; Base has two detected Gurobi
and thirteen CPLEX contradictions. These applicability limits, the known-parent
design and finite solver caps delimit the conclusions of this confirmation.
