\paragraph{Fresh exports with matched controls.}
The new replication uses ten seeds, 20260909--20260918, at each of three exponent
ranges, two severities and three coupling patterns: 180 generated instances and
1,080 exports. The corrected serializer preserves binary64 values on readback,
including coefficients, RHS values and bounds. All exports preserve named
structure and pass the whole-row proportionality check at relative tolerance
$10^{-13}$. This is a floating-point transformation audit, not an exact rational
equivalence certificate.

SA-Mat and Role-Hybrid are byte-identical on 136 of 180 instances; SA-Mat and
its permuted-scale counterpart are byte-identical on 165. The nonidentical cases
matter: the production implementation includes window vetoes and filtered block
extrema, so the exact-collapse hypotheses need not hold universally.
On the common-rank spectral diagnostic, the SA-Mat/Role-Hybrid ratio lies between
0.939 and 1.079, with median one. The scale-permutation ratio lies between
0.99962 and 1.00022. In contrast, the median SA-Mat/Flat-GM ratios are 0.937,
0.102 and 0.00973 as the exponent range grows from $[-1,1]$ to $[-3,3]$.
Thus the large heterogeneity-dependent advantage is explained by the coupling
kernel; the matched control reveals smaller implementation-dependent differences.

Numerical rank is fixed per instance from the row-L2-normalized Base at threshold
$10^{-12}$ and used for every variant. Independently thresholding each variant
would give different ranks in 25 of the 1,080 exports. Several unscaled matrices
approach binary64 resolution, so their very large condition estimates are not
interpreted quantitatively. The scaled matrices used in the paired ratios have
diagnostics below $6.5\times10^6$. Direct named-export comparisons carry the
identity claims and do not depend on singular-value estimation.
