     Identifiability and Feasibility Budgets in Pre-Export Row Scaling for
                            Mixed-Integer Programs
                                           Mathias Rodríguez Castro
                    Facultad de Ingeniería, Universidad de la República, Montevideo, Uruguay
                                              mathiasr@fing.edu.uy


                                                      Abstract
               Positive row scaling preserves a mixed-integer linear program in exact arithmetic, but an
          observed numerical improvement need not identify the information used to select the factors.
          We analyze a local–block–coupling construction and prove that geometric-mean row centering
          can erase its block-scale information exactly. With a near-identity band, the remaining scale
          information is bounded independently of the original heterogeneity. A matched kernel control
          explains an apparent order-of-magnitude spectral improvement without using block scales. We
          then characterize the positive row factors compatible with an explicit original-unit residual budget
          and a coefficient window. The admissible set is an interval; constrained geometric-mean centering
          is its logarithmic minimax solution, and an empty interval provides an explicit incompatibility
          certificate. An integer-rounding bound isolates an error that scaling cannot suppress, and a
          discrete characterization selects exactly representable power-of-two exports. A reconstruction
          of 240 dispatch runs separates completed-run effort from failures, and 3,456 configurations of
          exactly enumerable binary stress instances distinguish conditional row guarantees from solver
          behavior. Budget projection improves verification on HiGHS in this stress experiment, but does
          not establish a solver-independent advantage; a rounding-aware contract trades acceptance for
          conservative abstention. The results provide precise controls for attributing scaling gains and for
          stating what a numerical scaling policy can actually guarantee.

 1   Keywords: mixed-integer linear programming; row scaling; numerical reliability; equilibration;
 2   residual budgets; computational reproducibility.


 3   1    Introduction
 4   Model generators retain variable ownership, constraint roles and application units that a conventional
 5   matrix export may discard. Such information can guide scaling, but its presence in an algorithm does
 6   not establish that it affects the exported matrix. Row coverage, normalization kernels, safeguards
 7   and metadata can change together, confounding the interpretation of a numerical improvement.
 8       In mixed-integer linear programming (MILP), positive row scaling preserves the exact feasible
 9   set while changing the representation seen by presolve, relaxation solves and integer acceptance
10   tests. Coefficient magnitudes, matrix condition numbers, original-data residuals and running time
11   are therefore distinct outcomes. We study which generator information remains identifiable after
12   preprocessing and which numerical guarantees can be stated independently of solver behavior.
13       First, we analyze a local–block–coupling construction whose block statistic is the geometric mean
14   of the smallest and largest post-local coefficient magnitudes. Exact geometric-mean row centering
15   makes every such statistic one; an identity band permits only bounded variation. A matched
16   Euclidean coupling-row control then explains an apparent block-metadata benefit. Counterexamples
17   and direct export comparisons delimit the result when practical safeguards intervene.

                                                           1
18       Second, an original-unit residual budget supplies information that survives a change of row
19   units when transformed with the row. We characterize the factors that combine this budget with
20   coefficient limits, derive the constrained logarithmic minimax factor, and extend admissibility to
21   integer rounding and exact power-of-two export. An empty admissible set certifies incompatible
22   numerical specifications, not MILP infeasibility. Solver acceptance remains a separate question.
23       The evidence combines a controlled matrix audit, reconstructed dispatch measurements, and
24   exactly enumerable binary stress instances. Direct tolerance scaling and matched kernel controls
25   prevent crediting the proposed construction with benefits already explained by simpler rules. The
26   contribution is a characterization and reproducible audit of these mechanisms, with explicit limits
27   on the guarantees.


28   2    Related work
29   Diagonal scaling has a long history, including the logarithmic method of Curtis and Reid [3]. Elble
30   and Sahinidis [5] compare scalers for simplex and find that additional complexity need not improve
31   performance. Klotz [14] relates numerical instability to formulation and finite precision. Optimal
32   diagonal preconditioning is a broader problem: Qu et al. [17] use quasiconvex and semidefinite
33   formulations, while Demmel [4] gives block-Jacobi guarantees. Our triangular calculation is an
34   explanatory scalar special case, not a general preconditioning algorithm.
35       Robustness is also an established objective in MIP scaling. Berthold and Hendel [1] learn to
36   select between standard and Curtis–Reid scaling in Xpress from an indicator of numerical difficulties.
37   Their implementation uses power-of-two factors and their evaluation examines inconsistencies across
38   seeds. Our external construction instead states row-level admissibility conditions; it does not predict
39   or replace internal solver scaling.
40       Modeling environments already support generator-level information: Pyomo’s ScaleModel
41   applies component factors [16], GAMS has variable and equation scale attributes [6], and IDAES
42   derives and propagates scales across submodels [10, 11]. Plasmo.jl retains local problems and linking
43   constraints [13]. Consequently, using modeling metadata before export is not new. Our identifiability
44   result concerns the loss of one specific block statistic after a preceding normalization.
45       Tolerance-based scaling also has direct precedent. Gurobi documents absolute feasibility/integrality
46   tolerances and the treatment of very small coefficients [7], and recommends external scaling that
47   makes tolerance-sized violations negligible in application units [9]. Pritchett, Howell and Grebow [15]
48   similarly scale constraints using physical accuracy requirements and SNOPT’s tolerance. Section 5
49   combines this known transfer with coefficient limits, identifies incompatible specifications, and solves
50   the constrained centering problem. Integer rounding and exact binary64 export impose additional
51   restrictions.
52       Finally, Cheung, Gleixner and Steffy [2] provide certificates for integer-programming results, and
53   Hoen and Gleixner [8] analyze individual branch-and-bound decisions. Our rational residual checks
54   concern primal acceptance; independent enumeration establishes optimality only on the small binary
55   instances. The complementary attribution audit checks actual exports under matched kernels and
56   coverage. These contributions concern identifiable mechanisms and explicit numerical contracts,
57   without claiming universal performance superiority.

65       Let the generator provide a fixed partition of the local rows into nonempty blocks, a coupling-row
66   set, and a column-to-block map β. Coverage specifies which rows are eligible for scaling; a kernel
67   maps eligible row data to a candidate factor; safeguards decide which factors are applied. These are
68   separate choices. A block map can influence the factors even when coverage and kernels are fixed,

75   safeguards. Flat-L2 also changes the local kernel, whereas Flat-GM changes the coupling kernel.

77   assignment of unequal scales is a different intervention: it tests the usefulness of the map on the
78   chosen instances, and is not assumed to preserve semantic validity.


79   4     Identifying the contribution of structural metadata
80   An external scaling policy may use a row’s coverage status, a kernel selected by its role, and the
81   ownership of its columns. These are distinct inputs. A comparison identifies the contribution
82   of block ownership only when coverage, the row-role assignment, and the kernels remain fixed.
83   Matching the local kernel alone does not meet this condition. This distinction has a particularly
84   strong consequence for the post-normalization block statistic used by the original policy.

119   4.2   What a matched comparison identifies
120   With equal coverage and equal local kernels, two exports agree on their local rows. Their differences
121   are confined to the coupling rows. This elementary localization result does not establish that
122   the difference is due to block ownership: the coupling kernels may differ too. An appropriate
123   ownership control keeps local geometric-mean normalization and coupling Euclidean normalization,
124   but replaces all block scales by one. A separate role control compares this hybrid policy with
125   Euclidean normalization on every row. Comparing only with geometric-mean normalization on
126   every row mixes role-dependent kernel selection with ownership information.
127       Inactivity of one policy’s coupling stage does not imply equality with a flat policy. Consider
128   local rows x1 ≤ 1, x2 ≤ 1, and the coupling inequality x1 + 10−2 x2 ≤ 1. Both local blocks have scale
129   one. The Euclidean coupling candidate is (1 + 10−4 )−1/2 and is skipped by the band (1/2, 2). The
130   flat geometric-mean candidate is 10 and is applied. Both policies cover all rows, yet their coupling
131   exports differ. Equality requires equality of the actually applied factors, rather than inactivity of
132   just one policy.

162   5    Equilibration with an explicit residual budget
163   Block ownership is not the only information a generator can supply. An application can also state
164   how much violation of each original constraint is acceptable. Such a budget must come from the
165   application or be declared as a numerical test parameter; it cannot be inferred reliably from a zero
166   right-hand side or a block label. Scaling to make solver tolerances meaningful is established practice
167   [7, 11]. The contribution of the following construction is an explicit feasibility test and an optimality
168   characterization for combining that budget with coefficient limits, including a reason to abstain.
169       For a nonconstant row aT x ≤ b, define the original residual v(x) = (aT x − b)+ and specify a
170   budget δ > 0. Let ϵ > 0 be a hypothetical bound on the residual of the exported row. A solver’s
171   feasibility parameter alone does not establish this hypothesis: internal scaling, presolve, integrality
172   tolerances and finite precision intervene. For a positive row factor α, the identity
                                               (αaT x − αb)+ = αv(x)                                        (1)
173   holds in exact arithmetic. Consequently, the hypothesis of exported residual at most ϵ implies
174   v(x) ≤ δ whenever α ≥ ϵ/δ. Absolute residuals give the same identity for equality constraints.
175       Write m = minaj ̸=0 |aj | and M = maxj |aj |, and specify coefficient limits 0 < ℓ ≤ U . These

226   5.2   An exact export on a dyadic grid
227   Even when a factor satisfies the real-arithmetic interval, independently rounded products fl(αaj )
228   and fl(αb) need not describe an exactly proportional row over the stored data. Merely rounding a
229   computed endpoint to nearest can also move it outside a closed admissible interval. Our arbitrary-
230   factor implementation forms the interval endpoints as exact rational numbers in the supplied
231   binary64 inputs and rounds them inward; it abstains if the interval contains no positive binary64
232   factor. This certifies factor admissibility, not exactness of all subsequent products.
233       Powers of two provide an established way to avoid multiplication rounding. The following
234   explicit intersection also accounts for subnormal numbers and for the RHS. Assume IEEE binary64
235   arithmetic with gradual underflow [12]. Write each nonzero datum z ∈ {a1 , . . . , an , b} uniquely as
236   z = sign(z)Nz 2pz with Nz a positive odd integer, and put qz = ⌊log2 |z|⌋. Let [L, H] be the desired
237   real factor interval, including any rounding allowance from (6).

252   Verification after solving. The interval theorem is conditional and exact-arithmetic. Every
253   returned point must still be checked against the original rows, bounds and integrality restrictions.
254   Our small stress experiments compute row residuals by exact rational arithmetic on the supplied
255   binary64 coefficients and returned solution. They also enumerate the binary assignments to determine
256   the optimum independently. These checks are possible because those instances are deliberately small.
257   On large dispatch models the archived checker uses floating-point residuals, which are diagnostics
258   rather than a proof of MIP optimality. General certificates of branch-and-bound correctness address
259   a much stronger task [2].

261   6.1   Matrix attribution audit
262   The C++ generator constructs local blocks and coupling rows while retaining their row roles and
263   column ownership. The archived headline cell uses four heterogeneous blocks, coefficient exponent
264   range [−2, 2], coupling disturbance severity 6, and 30 seeds. All policies in table 1 are available
265   in the same implementation. Their exported matrices allow a comparison before any optimizer
266   is called. The analysis records spectral pseudo-condition numbers and direct export comparisons;
267   equal condition numbers alone are not evidence of identical matrices.
268       The spectral diagnostic is κ+                        +
                                         2 (A) = σmax (A)/σmin (A), where numerical rank is determined
269   relative to the largest singular value. It is a diagnostic of the exported constraint matrix, not of the
270   active basis at every node. Variable bounds, redundant rows and slack conventions can change its
271   interpretation. A condition-number ratio is never used as a proxy for verified solver success.

272   6.2   Operational reconstruction
273   The operational case study uses the final four-arm campaign from the dispatch repository: Base,
274   Flat-GM, Flat-L2 and Role-Hybrid; 15 instances from each of the Simple and SG-Ter-Mer families;
275   Gurobi; relative MIP gaps 1% and 0.1%; seed 42; 16 threads; and a 1,800-second solver limit. The
276   fixed subsets are retained rather than reselected after inspecting outcomes. This gives 240 assigned
277   runs. We retrieved all native logs from the cluster and preserved input hashes.
278       The primary runtime endpoint is arithmetic mean PAR10 over all 15 assigned instances per cell,
279   with 18,000 seconds assigned to a non-completion. This measures solver completion rather than
280   independently certified feasibility. Native logs recover Work at a common resolution of 0.01 units;
281   the archived runner had accidentally rounded scaled-variant markers to integers. Work is analyzed
282   separately on the common all-variants-completed pool, with exploratory paired bootstrap intervals.
283   Online Resource 1 specifies the reconstruction, resolution sensitivity and residual checks. The
284   selected pools and repeated instances across gap settings do not constitute independent replications
285   of general solver performance.

371   7.3     Conditional contracts and verified binary optima
372   The full counts appear in Online Resource 1 (Table S2). Budget-GM verifies 182 of 192 configurations
373   on HiGHS, compared with 141 for GM and 167 for Base. Budget-only verifies 191: direct budget
374   scaling is more successful than the extra minimax centering in this experiment. On Gurobi,
375   Budget-GM verifies 152, compared with 140 for GM and 156 for Base. GM-tight verifies 166 under
376   each solver. Thus the row-budget construction does not establish a solver-independent reliability
377   improvement, and its conditional hypothesis must remain visible.
378       Dyadic-GM verifies 142 configurations on HiGHS and 140 on Gurobi; Budget-dyadic verifies 161
379   and 153, respectively. Exact representation of the scaled row is therefore insufficient for reliable
380   solver acceptance on these stress cases. In a preserved earlier implementation, rounding the budget
381   endpoint to nearest instead of inward changed HiGHS Budget-GM’s verified count from 182 to 191.
382   This is a numerical sensitivity result, not a reason to prefer a factor that fails its declared interval.
383   The artifact preserves both implementations’ outputs and identifies the corrected run.
384       Budget-round accepts and verifies 48 configurations per solver, and abstains on the other 144.
385   Its zero observed verification failures have a substantial cost: it declines many instances that
386   another policy solves correctly. This is expected of a worst-case rounding bound. An incompatibility
387   certificate explains why the requested uniform contract cannot be asserted; it is not evidence that

389   cannot masquerade as perfect overall performance.


390   8    Discussion and limits
391   The collapse theorem concerns extrema-based summaries after geometric-mean normalization. It
392   does not rule out useful variable scales, physical tolerances or other block statistics. Its practical
393   implication is to hold coverage, kernels and safeguards fixed before attributing an effect to block
394   information, and to inspect the actual row factors or named exports. Safeguards can introduce
395   discontinuities; equal scalar diagnostics or rounded Work values cannot prove export identity.
396       Residual budgets give a distinct interpretation to generator information. Their joint compatibility
397   with coefficient limits is explicit and invariant under consistent row re-emission. The contract remains
398   conditional: integer-rounding amplification and internal solver transformations can defeat predictions
399   based on the exported row alone. Production use requires a postsolve acceptance or repair policy
400   and measurement of its full cost.
401       The dispatch reconstruction covers two application classes, one solver, one seed and 15 selected
402   instances per class. The new budget policies have not been validated there using application-
403   supplied engineering tolerances. The matrix audit tests an algebraic mechanism; the binary
404   instances deliberately amplify tolerance sensitivity. Neither estimates typical industrial failure
405   rates or speedups. Enumeration certifies the small-instance optima, with no corresponding exact
406   optimality claim for dispatch.


407   9    Conclusion
408   For the studied local–block–coupling construction, geometric-mean row centering can remove all
409   information from a subsequent extrema-based block-scale summary. A bounded identity band
410   only restores bounded scale variation. This explains why changing a coupling-row kernel can look
411   like a block-metadata benefit when the comparison does not hold kernels fixed. The operational
412   reconstruction further shows that favorable completed-run effort can coexist with an unfavorable
413   failure-sensitive runtime summary.
414        An explicit residual budget provides a separate, interpretable basis for row scaling. Its compati-
415   bility with coefficient limits can be characterized exactly, and constrained logarithmic centering has
416   a closed-form solution. Integer-rounding amplification and solver-internal transformations limit what
417   this conditional contract can guarantee. The resulting recommendation is to expose the information
418   used, audit the actual transformation, declare incompatible specifications, and verify the returned
419   point.
