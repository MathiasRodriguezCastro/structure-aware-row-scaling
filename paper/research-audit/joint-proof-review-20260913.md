## Statements checked and their boundaries

1. **Cell separation.** The error box is nonempty and compact. Its linear image and the translated downward orthant are disjoint exactly when a strictly separating nonnegative normal exists. The separation direction and strict sign agree with the row-gap implementation. Equality of the maximum margin to zero is not a certificate of exclusion.

2. **Finite rays.** The two-row statement must ignore columns that are zero in both rows; otherwise its literal wording would describe infinitely many cancellation points. For m rows, intersect the nonnegative unit-sum simplex with column cancellation hyperplanes. The objective is affine on each sign cell, so a maximizing vertex exists. A support-s vertex needs s-1 independent cancellations. Enumerating supports and columns gives at most sum_s C(m,s) C(n,s-1)=C(n+m,m-1) candidate systems. Singular systems are ignored, not inverted approximately. This is classical polyhedral geometry applied to the numerical contract.

3. **Weighted residual threshold.** Normalization lambda*w=1 makes the multiplier domain compact because every weight is positive. Bilinear minimax over the multiplier simplex and the error box gives min_e max_r residual_r/w_r. On each unit-sum sign cell, numerator/denominator is a convex combination of vertex ratios, with positive reweighted coefficients. Thus the same finite rays attain the exact threshold. The strict acceptance boundary follows from compactness; negative and zero thresholds are intrinsic collisions when the box uses exact bounds. If there are no bad centers the threshold is treated as infinity (represented by `None`), not zero.

4. **Remediability.** An exact feasible point in a bad cell survives every positive row scaling and all LP-valid inequalities. Conversely, finitely many disjoint compact cells each have a positive minimax residual; the minimum is positive. Arbitrarily large finite row scaling can then exclude all cells. A finite coefficient ceiling changes the test to the maximum admissible scaling vector, by monotonicity. This does not guarantee that a floating-point optimizer actually honors the exported contract or proves the correct bound.

5. **Multirow coNP hardness.** The SAT construction has a known feasible all-zero point. Bad cells must round the gate to one. An unsatisfied clause has literal sum at most 3*eta, contradicting gate value at least 1-eta when eta<1/4. A rational vertex gives a polynomial-size complement witness. Copying variable occurrences and gates along exact equality chains keeps rows at most four-sparse and columns at most five-sparse. Exact equalities force identical rounded copies for eta<1/2. This construction's obstruction is removed immediately by integer bound tightening; no hard-to-reformulate claim follows.

6. **One-row contrast.** Complementing negative columns reduces the problem to positive weights and a shifted RHS. Intrinsic collision is precisely a subset weight in (B,B/(1-eta)]. Partitioning items at the interval width permits enumeration of fewer than 1/eta large items, followed by greedy insertion of small items at the first strict crossing. The overshoot is at most the interval width. This proves the fixed-eta polynomial bound; its enormous exponent is explicitly acknowledged. When eta is input, B=T-1 and eta=1/T give interval (T-1,T], yielding a reduction from SUBSET SUM with integer RHS. Computing the exact residual threshold is a different problem and contains the minimum subset sum above B even when eta is fixed.
