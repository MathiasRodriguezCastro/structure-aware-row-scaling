\noindent To the Editors of \emph{Mathematical Programming Computation}

\bigskip
\noindent Dear Editors,

\medskip
Please consider the manuscript ``Pre-Export Row Scaling for Generated Mixed-Integer Programs:
Mechanism Attribution and Residual-Budget Contracts'' for publication in \emph{Mathematical
Programming Computation}.

Multiplying a row of a mixed-integer program by a positive number preserves it exactly while
changing the numbers a solver sees. The paper asks what that change buys and what it still
guarantees. The first half attributes an observed gain: holding one LP basis fixed per model
across representations, geometric-mean row scaling lowers the basis condition number on all 306
generated dispatch models by six to eight orders of magnitude, and an advantage previously read
as evidence for block metadata proves to be a coupling-kernel effect that a control using no
ownership information reproduces. The second half states what a representation can guarantee:
residual budgets in application units survive consistent re-emission, and the factors compatible
with a budget and coefficient limits are characterized exactly, together with an incompatibility
certificate, the integer-rounding term that scaling cannot remove, and exact binary64 export.

The submission fits this journal because the computations, not only their summaries, are part of
what is offered: a fixed-basis audit of 306 models with an independent reconstruction of all
1{,}530 bases and certified rational bounds on a stress sample; 1{,}080 matched matrix exports;
240 operational solver runs with two matched sensitivity reruns of the same size, changing one
factor at a time; and 4{,}752 optimizer calls across HiGHS, Gurobi and CPLEX in which every
reported optimum is verified against an independent enumeration of the stored models.

The paper claims no universal scaling advantage, and several results run against the convenient
reading. The completed-run gain measured on one dispatch class survives a change of seed and
disappears under a change of solver. The single instance that reverses the failure-sensitive
ordering behaves differently in every configuration tested. Plain coefficient centering loses
verified optima relative to the unscaled model on all three solvers, attaching a residual budget
to the same rule loses none relative to that rule, and whether scaling helps at all differs
across the three pipelines.

Online Resource~1 holds the proof for the triangular model, the audit protocols and complete
ranges, the reconstruction and experiment protocols, and the reproduction instructions. Online
Resource~2 is the source and data archive: exporter, analysis and verification scripts, stored
experiment data with manifests and checksums, and tests. The figures, the fixed-basis audit, the
verification of the saved binary solutions and the HiGHS stress arm need no commercial licence;
only the Gurobi and CPLEX arms do.

I am the sole author, received no external funding and declare no competing interests. The
manuscript discloses the use of AI assistance and states that the reported numbers come from the
stored runs and that I am responsible for the scientific content. It is not under consideration
elsewhere and has not been published before.
