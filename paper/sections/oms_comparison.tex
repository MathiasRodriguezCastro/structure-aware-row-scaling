\subsection{A controlled comparison with row-only Ruiz}
\label{subsec:results-ruiz}
Table~\ref{tab:oms-comparison} compares four representations under the compact matched
protocol of \cref{subsec:design-oms}. Every policy uses the same inputs and solver
settings. The condition numbers describe named fixed bases of the exported models;
the equilibrated column reports the same diagnostic after the documented transformation,
not an inspection of Gurobi's internal matrices. Work and time describe completed
runs, whereas PAR10 retains every failure at the declared limit. The physical checks
use saved incumbents, including those from unfinished optimization calls, and do not
turn completion at a 1\% gap into an independent optimality certificate.

On the 14 commonly completed SG-Ter-Mer models, the paired work ratios are 0.643 for
Flat-GM, 0.575 for Role-Hybrid and 0.954 for Ruiz-R4; total-time ratios are respectively
0.659, 0.628 and 1.121. All four policies time out on instance077 at this shorter limit.
Mean PAR10 is consequently compressed to 95.3 seconds for Base, 91.7 for Flat-GM,
88.6 for Role-Hybrid and 95.2 for Ruiz-R4. On Simple, Ruiz-R4 uses slightly less work
(0.928), but its total-time ratio is 1.219; Flat-GM and Role-Hybrid are close to Base.

Ruiz-R4 has the smallest median paired condition ratio after equilibration on both classes,
0.059 on Simple and 0.072 on SG-Ter-Mer. Before equilibration its SG-Ter-Mer ratio is
1.3, whereas GM-based policies reach $7.3\times10^{-9}$: even this controlled comparison
changes its conditioning ordering after equilibration, and that ordering does not predict
the work ranking. All 120 saved comparison incumbents meet the demanding physical budgets.
Whole-model activity-normalized residual diagnostics are at most $1.2\times10^{-7}$,
bound violations at most $1.7\times10^{-7}$ and integer deviations at most
$3.9\times10^{-7}$; these diagnostics complement the exact physical-row audit.

Nine of the fifteen SG-Ter-Mer scenarios have the documented total-demand inversion.
Restricting this same campaign to the six without it (instance001, instance005,
instance022, instance027, instance031 and instance046), all four policies complete.
Paired work/time ratios to Base are 0.473/0.488 for Flat-GM, 0.472/0.502 for
Role-Hybrid and 1.240/1.455 for Ruiz-R4. These saved-run sensitivity results
preserve the conditional efficiency finding on that subset; they do not broaden
the diversity of generators or certify dispatch optimality.

This experiment tests competitiveness against a known row-equilibration kernel without
changing the external variable space. Its bounded local configuration adds a controlled
comparison to the earlier evidence; its ratios and times are not pooled with the
1,800-second experiment or interpreted as universal method rankings.
