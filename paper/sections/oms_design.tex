\subsection{Controlled row-only Ruiz comparison and final-policy ablation}
\label{subsec:design-oms}
An additional compact experiment uses exactly the 30 operational inputs, including
instance077 and the scenarios affected by the documented input defect. It compares Base,
Flat-GM, Role-Hybrid and Ruiz-R4 through fresh solver-free LP exports imported directly
by Gurobi. This import path is part of the additional protocol and is kept separate from
the historical generator-to-solver campaign. Ruiz-R4 is
a coefficient-only, row-only restriction of Ruiz's
infinity-norm iteration \cite{ruiz2001scaling}. Starting with $d_r=1$ and
$M_r=\max_j|a_{rj}|$, each of four half-steps multiplies $d_r$ and the current row by
$M_r^{-1/2}$; columns remain fixed. The cumulative candidate is therefore
$M_r^{-15/16}$. It passes the same closed identity band $[1/2,2]$ and coefficient-window
veto $[10^{-9},10^9]$ as the final kernels, applied once to the cumulative candidate.
The RHS is multiplied by the accepted factor but does not enter the norm.
This restricted control is neither the full row-and-column algorithm nor the historical
C++ option that combines Ruiz iterations with block normalization.

The prospective protocol fixes Gurobi 13.0.2, four threads, seed 42, a 1\% relative gap,
120 seconds, \texttt{FeasibilityTol}$=10^{-6}$, \texttt{IntFeasTol}$=10^{-5}$,
and default presolve and internal scaling. A separate six-call, 60-second cost pilot is
excluded. Calls run sequentially on an Intel i7-13620H, with a fixed shuffled policy order
within each instance. Total time includes measured generator/export time, the Ruiz
transformation where applicable, model loading and optimization; checking is excluded.
Work and total-time ratios use the subset completed by all four policies in each class.
Completion counts and mean PAR10 use all 15, assigning 1,200 seconds to an unfinished run.
These local measurements are kept separate from the 1,800-second campaign above.

Conditioning uses the stored named Base LP basis for each input, verified against the
new Base export by SHA-256. Structural columns use each policy's own coefficients;
basic slacks remain unit columns. We report the largest 1-norm inverse estimate across
seeds 0, 1 and 2, before and after ten passes of the documented power-of-two
equilibration. Named full-matrix checks verify row proportionality, RHS, objective,
bounds and integer domains before optimization.

The stage ablation uses positions 1, 8 and 15 of each archived instance list, six models
in total, without outcome-based selection. Two additional exports disable respectively
the coupling and local stage of the final Role-Hybrid implementation. Their nonunit row
supports are disjoint and their factors multiply to the full-policy factors within the
serialization tolerance. Base and full-policy runs are shared with the comparison.
The resulting 132 calls and their stored incumbents support original-row, bound and
integrality checks. Physical verification additionally uses exact rational arithmetic
on the original binary64 coefficients and the returned binary64 point; it certifies
primal residuals for those stored data, not optimality of the dispatch MILP.

Three application-precision scenarios are fixed before the campaign. Power and water
budgets are 0.01\,MW and 100\,m\textsuperscript{3} (demanding),
0.1\,MW and 1{,}000\,m\textsuperscript{3} (reference), and
1\,MW and 10{,}000\,m\textsuperscript{3} (permissive). These are study objectives, not UTE
operating tolerances: the reference expresses power to a tenth of a MW and water to
a thousandth of a cubic hectometre, with one-decade sensitivity in either direction.
Each physical budget is converted to algebraic row units using the coefficient of
the named generation or storage variable, or the 3,600-second hourly flow conversion.
The audit covers power identities and demand caps, plant generation definitions,
production envelopes and water balances; other rows have no declared physical budget.
For each row and scenario it retains the proposed factor, inward-rounded admissible
interval, projected factor and any incompatibility. The interval audit uses zero
rounding allowance and the conditional exported-row hypothesis $\epsilon=10^{-6}$;
actual residuals and pointwise coefficient/RHS export defects are checked separately.
The physical checks use the raw stored incumbent without rounding integer coordinates;
contracts for rounded assignments require the separate allowance in
\eqref{eq:rounding-budget}.

A separate exploratory activation follow-up uses the six archived SG-Ter-Mer models
without the documented demand inversion, seeds 1, 7 and 42, and the same 120-second
local limit. It applies the previously declared numerical volume goals to activation
thresholds, rather than treating them as operator tolerances. L2 with coefficient-window
projection and Budget-L2 share a budget-guided integer allowance; the global-tight control
uses primal and integer tolerances of $10^{-9}$. Additional mechanism controls and a
presolve-off comparison on three fresh seeds are kept separate. Exact witnesses are
obtained by fixing observed integer states and solving the remaining LP with SoPlex
\cite{gleixner2016refinement,gleixner2020oracles}, then checking every original binary64
row, bound and integer domain independently with rational arithmetic. These are feasible
points, not global MILP optimality certificates. Supplementary Material~1 gives the full
protocol, native-export identities and preparation-cost measurements.
